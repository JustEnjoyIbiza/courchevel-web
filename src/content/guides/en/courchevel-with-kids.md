## Ski school by age

Courchevel has several ski schools, and the École du Ski Français (ESF) has schools in several of its villages. At ESF Courchevel 1850, children are grouped by age:

| Age | What is offered |
| --- | --- |
| 18 months to 3 years | Nursery groups, a first introduction to the snow |
| 3 to 5 years | The children's village, a protected snow garden where lessons are built around games |
| 6 to 12 years | Group lessons by level |
| 13 to 17 years | Teen groups |

In 1650 Moriond, the ESF runs a Club Piou-Piou for children aged 3 to 5, and a Mini-Piou course for 2 to 3 year olds outside French school holidays. Group sizes, schedules and minimum numbers change from season to season, so we confirm the details for your dates when we book.

For a shy child, a first week on skis, or a family that wants to ski together, a private instructor is often the gentler choice. Private lessons can be booked by the hour or for the day, and the same instructor can stay with your children for the whole week. We book group lessons and private instructors through our [ski services](/services/ski/).

## Lift passes for young children

Children under 5 ski on a free lift pass. It is collected at the lift ticket office on presentation of an identity document showing the child's date of birth, so bring a passport or identity card for each young child. Children in the youngest ski school groups often stay within the snow garden and do not need a pass at all, but the school will confirm this when you book.

From age 5, children need a pass at the child rate. We arrange passes for the whole family before you arrive, so nobody queues on the first morning; we quote them for your dates.

## Childcare: day care and nannies

Courchevel has two municipal day-care centres, Les Marmots in Moriond and La Tanière des Croès in La Tania. Both take children from 6 months to 4 years, subject to places, and according to the tourist office they open Sunday to Friday. Places go quickly in the school holidays, and registration is through the town hall.

A private nanny gives you more flexibility. A nanny can look after a baby in the chalet while older children are in ski school, take children to and from lessons, and stay for the evening so that parents can go out. For families with several young children, many choose a nanny for the whole stay. Tell us the ages of your children, the hours you need and any languages that matter to you, and we propose a nanny through our [childcare service](/services/childcare/).

## Activities for children who do not ski

Not every child wants to ski every day. Courchevel's family programme covers a wide range:

- **Sledging:** a 2 km sledging run through the forest from Courchevel 1850 down to Courchevel Village, a 3 km run at Courchevel Moriond, and dedicated children's sledging areas in 1850 and La Tania. The tourist office recommends a helmet.
- **Swimming:** Aquamotion in Courchevel 1550 has water slides, a wave pool and a separate wellness area for adults.
- **Skating:** the Forum in Courchevel 1850 has an Olympic-size indoor ice rink.
- **With animals:** pony rides and dog sledding.
- **In the evenings:** torchlight descents by the ski schools, ski shows and children's events through the season.

We can also arrange private activities at the chalet, from a snowman-building afternoon to a children's cooking class with your chef. See our [experiences](/services/experiences/).

## Which village suits your family

Each village has a different pace:

- **Courchevel 1850:** the most hotels, shops and restaurants, and the altiport. Busy in the holidays, with lively evenings.
- **1650 Moriond:** sunny, family-oriented slopes, with ski school and day care in the village.
- **1550 Village:** close to Aquamotion and linked to 1850 by gondola, with a quieter feel.
- **1300 Le Praz:** a traditional village at the edge of the forest, with lifts up to 1850.
- **La Tania:** a smaller village set among the trees, with its own day-care centre and a snow front for children.

For young children, being a short walk from the ski school meeting point matters more than anything else. We look for chalets with that in mind.

## A typical family day

Most families settle into a simple rhythm after the first morning. Children go to ski school or to their instructor at around nine, while parents ski on their own or with a guide. A nanny or a parent collects the youngest at lunchtime; older children often stay on for an afternoon lesson. Lunch can be back at the chalet, which suits toddlers who need a nap, or on a terrace close to the ski school meeting point.

Afternoons are when non-skiing activities fit most easily: a session at the pool, an hour on the sledging run, or skating before dinner. In the evening, an early supper for the children with the chalet chef, followed by a quieter dinner for the adults, is a pattern many families enjoy. If you would like to go out, a nanny can stay with the children for the evening. We plan this with you before you arrive, so the first day already runs smoothly.

## Practical tips for families

- **Book ski school early.** For the Christmas and February holidays, places in the youngest groups fill first.
- **Bring identity documents** for every child under 5, for the free lift pass.
- **Plan rest days.** Altitude and cold tire young children; an afternoon at the pool or a quiet day in the chalet helps.
- **Ask for baby equipment in advance.** We place cots, high chairs, baby baths, stair gates and car seats in the chalet before you arrive.
- **Helmets** are strongly recommended for every child on skis or sledges.
