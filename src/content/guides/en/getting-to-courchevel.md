## Travel times at a glance

Courchevel sits at the top of a single access road above Moûtiers, in the Tarentaise valley of Savoie. Most guests fly into Geneva, Lyon or Chambéry and finish the journey by car or helicopter. The times below are approximate and assume normal conditions; Saturday traffic in high season and snowfall can add a great deal to the road times.

| Route | By car | By helicopter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geneva airport | 149 km, about 2 h 15 to 2 h 45 | about 30 to 35 min |
| Lyon Saint-Exupéry | 187 km, about 2 h 20 to 2 h 50 | about 40 to 45 min |
| Chambéry airport | 110 km, about 1 h 20 to 1 h 30 | about 25 min |
| Milan | 4 h 30 to 5 h, via the Fréjus or Mont Blanc tunnel | 50 min to 1 h |
| Moûtiers station | 25 km, about 30 min | not usually needed |

Helicopter times are operators' typical flight times between landing sites and do not include the short transfers at each end.

## By car

The motorway runs most of the way. From Geneva and Lyon the route joins the A43 and then the N90 dual carriageway up the Tarentaise valley to Moûtiers, where the mountain road climbs to Courchevel's villages: Le Praz, La Tania, 1550 Village, 1650 Moriond and 1850.

Saturdays in the French and British school holidays are the slowest days of the winter, because most chalet and apartment rentals change over that day. If you can arrive on another day, the journey is usually far calmer. A private chauffeured transfer lets you rest, make calls or keep the children comfortable on the way. See our [private transfers](/services/transfers/).

### Winter driving law

From 1 November 2026 to 31 March 2027, the mountain law (Loi Montagne II) applies in the communes listed by the Savoie prefecture, which include Courchevel. In these zones, cars must either have four winter tyres marked with the 3PMSF symbol (a mountain with a snowflake) or carry chains or snow socks able to fit at least two driving wheels. The zones are marked by road signs at their entry and exit. Hire cars from Geneva are not always fitted for French mountain roads, so it is worth checking with the rental company before you leave.

## By train

The nearest station is Moûtiers-Salins-Brides-les-Bains, usually shortened to Moûtiers, about 30 minutes by road below the resort.

- **From London:** in winter 2026/27 the Eurostar ski service runs on Saturdays from 19 December 2026 to 20 February 2027, from London St Pancras with a change at Lille Europe. Return trains run from 27 December 2026 to 28 February 2027, mostly on Sundays. The journey takes about eight hours. Note that the service ends in February this winter, earlier than in previous seasons.
- **From Paris:** TGV journeys to Moûtiers take roughly four and a half hours, with direct trains on some winter days and a change at Chambéry on others. Check SNCF Connect for your date.

We meet you on the platform at Moûtiers with a car and take your luggage, so the last half hour is easy.

## By helicopter

A helicopter is the quickest way in, and the most reliable way to avoid Saturday traffic. Flights land at Courchevel Altiport, a short drive above the chalets and hotels of 1850. From Geneva, a helicopter turns a drive of more than two hours into about half an hour in the air; from Milan, where the drive through the Fréjus or Mont Blanc tunnel takes four and a half to five hours, the flight takes 50 minutes to an hour. Every route, with indicative prices, is on our [transfers page](/services/transfers/).

Helicopters fly by sight in the mountains, so low cloud or heavy snow can delay or cancel a flight. When we arrange a helicopter, we keep a car ready as a back-up.

Heli-skiing drops are not allowed in France. If you would like to ski from a helicopter, we arrange day trips to Italy or Switzerland; see our [ski services](/services/ski/).

## By private plane and Courchevel Altiport

Courchevel Altiport is one of the most unusual airfields in Europe. Its runway is 536 metres long, sits at about 2,008 metres above sea level and climbs at a gradient of up to 18.5%. There is no instrument approach and no runway lighting, and the terrain rules out a go-around, so every landing has to be committed and made in good visibility.

Because of this, aeroplane pilots need the French mountain rating or a specific authorisation for Courchevel Altiport. In practice the altiport takes helicopters and propeller aircraft up to 5.7 tonnes, such as the Pilatus PC-12, and only flights from inside the Schengen area: a plane from London cannot land there directly.

**Private jets do not land at Courchevel.** They use Chambéry Savoie Mont Blanc, which has a dedicated business aviation terminal, or Geneva. From either airport we arrange the last leg by helicopter or by car, timed to your landing. If you fly privately, tell us your handler and arrival time and we coordinate the rest.

## Planning your arrival

A few things make the first day easier:

- **Arrive outside Saturday** if your chalet allows it, especially in the Christmas and February holidays.
- **Send us your flight or train details** once they are confirmed, so the driver or pilot can follow any delay.
- **Tell us about children** so the car has the right seats.
- **Send skis and heavy luggage ahead** if you are travelling by helicopter, where baggage space is limited.
