## About this example

This page shows one possible week, so you can picture how the pieces fit together. The group is invented for the example: two couples travelling together with two children aged 6 and 9, staying in a chalet in Courchevel 1850. Nothing here is a fixed programme. A couple on their own, a large group of friends or a family with a baby would have a very different week, and so would a stay at Christmas compared with one in January.

What does not change is the way we work: before you arrive, we prepare everything that can be prepared, and during the stay we adjust the plan to the weather, the snow and how everyone feels.

## Before you arrive

Most of the work happens in the weeks before the stay. We find the [chalet](/services/chalets/) that fits the group, close to the ski school meeting point for the children, and collect everyone's details:

- **Ski passes** for the six days, so nobody queues on the first morning.
- **Ski school** for the children and a **private instructor** for the adults who want one.
- **Equipment sizes,** so skis and boots can be fitted at the chalet.
- **Allergies and food preferences** for the chef and for every restaurant booking.
- **The kitchen stocked** to the group's list, from breakfast to the bar.

We also book the restaurant tables that matter most for the dates. In the festive weeks and in February, the most popular tables go early.

## Day 1: arrival by helicopter

The group lands at Geneva and flies to Courchevel Altiport, about 30 to 35 minutes in the air, while a car takes the luggage. A driver waits at the altiport to take everyone to the chalet. Flights only operate in daylight and suitable weather, so a road transfer of about 2 h 15 is on standby all day.

In the late afternoon, skis and boots are fitted at the chalet. Dinner is cooked at home by the [private chef](/services/private-chef/): an early meal for the children, then a relaxed dinner for the adults by the fire.

## Day 2: first morning on the snow

The children meet their ski school on the snow front at the Croisette, in the centre of the village. The adults spend the morning with a private instructor on the slopes above 1850, which is a gentle way to find their legs again.

Lunch is on the terrace of Le Chalet de Pierres, on the Verdons slope, a long-standing address for Savoyard cooking. In the afternoon, a massage therapist sets up at the chalet for recovery treatments, one after another, while the nanny takes the children skating at the Forum ice rink.

## Day 3: the Trois Vallées

With everyone settled, the adults ski further. From the Saulire, the slopes continue towards Méribel and the rest of the Trois Vallées, around 600 km of pistes in all. The instructor plans the route and the timing back, and we book lunch on the way.

In the evening, the adults dine at one of the starred restaurants of 1850, booked weeks before; our guide to [where to eat in Courchevel](/guides/where-to-eat/) lists them for this season. A nanny stays at the chalet with the children, and a driver brings everyone home.

## Day 4: a slower day

Altitude and cold tire everyone by the middle of the week, so this day is lighter. The children have their morning lesson, then the whole group takes the Grangettes gondola down to Courchevel 1550 Village for an afternoon at Aquamotion, with its pools and slides for the children and a wellness area for the adults.

Those who prefer to stay on snow take the 2 km sledging run through the forest from 1850 down to the Village. Dinner is back at the chalet with the chef.

## Day 5: off-piste with a mountain guide

For the strongest skiers, a certified mountain guide leads a day off the pistes, choosing the slopes according to the snow and the avalanche bulletin, with transceivers, shovels and probes checked before setting out.

Guests who want to go further can plan a heli-skiing day in Italy's Aosta Valley instead. Heli-skiing is not allowed in France, so it means an early start through the Mont Blanc Tunnel and a day with an Italian guide, confirmed on the morning according to the weather. Our [heli-skiing guide](/guides/heli-skiing-from-courchevel/) explains how a day in Italy or Switzerland works. The rest of the group skis with the instructor and meets for lunch on a terrace.

## Day 6: an evening out

The afternoon is for an activity off the skis. In this example, the group goes dog sledding at dusk, as passengers on forest trails, which suits the children as well as the adults. We arrange the transfer and check the provider's minimum age before booking.

After a short rest, the adults go out: après-ski, a late dinner in the village, then a table at a nightclub, with a driver for the whole evening. A babysitter stays at the chalet, and the next morning's lessons start a little later. See [nightlife](/services/nightlife/) for how we plan an evening like this.

## Day 7: a last day in the air and at the chalet

A scenic helicopter flight from the altiport, from about 15 minutes to an hour, over the Trois Vallées or towards Mont Blanc, is a calm way to see the mountains on the last morning. Flights are operated by licensed aviation partners.

The children finish the week with their ski school, and the final dinner is at the chalet: the chef prepares a tasting menu, and a birthday cake or a particular bottle can be ready without anyone having to ask.

## Departure

On the last morning the group leaves by car or helicopter. Snow and Saturday traffic can lengthen the road to Geneva, so we set the departure time around the flight home and keep watching the conditions.

## How the week changes with the season

- **Christmas and New Year:** the busiest fortnight. The chalet, the 24 and 31 December dinners and ski school for young children need booking as early as possible. See [Christmas and New Year’s Eve in Courchevel](/christmas-new-year-courchevel/).
- **January:** quieter, cold and often with very good snow. Restaurants can usually be booked a few days ahead.
- **February:** the French holidays run in three zones and overlap with the UK half-term, so the resort is full for a month. Plan the important dinners before you arrive.
- **March:** calmer again, with longer, sunnier days and lunches on the terraces.

Our guide to [season dates and events](/guides/season-dates-and-events/) has the full calendar for 2026/27.
