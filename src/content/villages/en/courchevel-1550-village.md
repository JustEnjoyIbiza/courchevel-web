## Who it suits

Courchevel Village sits directly below 1850, in a central position in the valley. It is a discreet place with a peaceful atmosphere and a long-established local community, and it has some of the larger family chalets in the resort.

It suits families who want room to spread out, groups who prefer quiet evenings at the chalet, and anyone who likes to be close to the action without staying in the middle of it. Many of the chalets here sit close to the pistes, which makes the start of each day straightforward. With the gondola to 1850 a few minutes away, an evening in the restaurants above is easy to arrange.

On a day off the slopes, Aquamotion, the resort's large aquatic and wellness centre, is a short walk or shuttle ride away, between the Village and Moriond. It is a useful option for children on a grey afternoon, and for adults who want a pool, sauna and treatments without leaving the valley.

## Getting there

**By road.** Courchevel Village is on the road up to the resort. As a guide, the resort is about 150 km from Geneva airport (2 h 15), 187 km from Lyon Saint-Exupéry (2 h to 2 h 15) and 110 km from Chambéry (1 h 20). Moûtiers, the nearest railway station, is around 30 minutes below the resort. On busy Saturdays and snowy days the drive takes longer, and we plan the timing accordingly.

**By air.** Courchevel Altiport, above 1850, takes helicopters and small aircraft in daylight and suitable weather. A car brings you down to the Village in a few minutes.

**Between villages.** The Grangettes gondola links Courchevel Village with 1850, and free shuttles connect all the villages on the road. Moriond is close by, and Le Praz is a short drive or ski away.

## Skiing from the village

The Village has direct access to the ski area. The Grangettes gondola takes you up to 1850, where the main lifts towards the Saulire and the high slopes begin, and pistes come back down through the forest to the village. From 1850 you can continue to Méribel and the rest of the Trois Vallées.

For families, the gondola makes it simple to reach the ski school meeting points in 1850 and come back down for lunch or a rest. We book lessons, passes and equipment in advance, so the first morning is about skiing rather than queues.

## Where to eat

The Village has a small, relaxed choice of restaurants. La Cortona, below the gondolas, serves lunch and dinner, and L'Oeil de Boeuf is known for meat grilled over a wood fire in a traditional chalet setting. There are also bars for après-ski along the main street.

For a more formal evening, the Michelin-starred restaurants of 1850 are a short gondola ride or drive away, and we can arrange the table and the car. Many guests in the Village also enjoy dinners at the chalet with a [private chef](/services/private-chef/), especially with children.
