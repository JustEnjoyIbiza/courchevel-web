## Méribel as seen from Courchevel

Méribel lies in the central valley of the Trois Vallées, between Courchevel to the east and the Belleville valley, with Val Thorens, to the south. For guests staying in Courchevel it is the closest neighbour on the ski map: a single ridge separates the two resorts, and lifts on both sides make the crossing part of an ordinary ski day.

The resort is spread along the valley. Méribel itself, around 1,450 m, is the main village, built in a consistent chalet style of wood and stone with slate roofs. Méribel-Mottaret, higher up at around 1,750 m, sits closer to the lifts towards Courchevel and Val Thorens. Smaller hamlets lie lower down the valley. In February 2023 Méribel and Courchevel jointly hosted the Alpine World Ski Championships.

## Getting there from Courchevel

**On skis.** From [Courchevel 1850](/courchevel-1850/), the lifts climb to La Saulire, at about 2,740 m, from where long pistes run down into the Méribel valley towards Mottaret and Méribel. From 1850, Le Praz or [La Tania](/la-tania/), the Col de la Loze offers a second crossing, which arrives above Méribel village. Both routes need a Trois Vallées pass. Plan the return carefully: the lifts back to Courchevel close in the late afternoon, and missing the last one means a long journey by road.

**By road.** The drive between the two resorts goes down to Moûtiers and back up the Méribel valley. From Courchevel 1850 the journey is about 45 km, and from Moûtiers the last 18 km climb the D90 to Méribel. Allow around an hour in good conditions, more on busy Saturdays or after heavy snow. Winter tyres or chains are compulsory in Savoie's mountain areas from 1 November to 31 March, and every car we book is equipped. See [getting to Courchevel](/guides/getting-to-courchevel/) for travel to the valley itself.

Further up the same network, [Val Thorens](/val-thorens/) is the highest resort in the Trois Vallées.

## Who Méribel suits

Méribel suits groups who want a lively village centre within walking distance of their chalet, families who like its central position for exploring the Trois Vallées, and skiers who want to reach both Courchevel and Val Thorens in a day. It also works well for a split stay, with some of the group in Méribel and others in Courchevel, joined each day on the slopes or for dinner.

Guests who prefer a calmer base between the two valleys sometimes choose La Tania or [Courchevel 1550 Village](/courchevel-1550-village/) instead, and visit Méribel for the day.

## Where to eat

Méribel's most acclaimed table is L'Ekrin by Laurent Azoulay, at the Hôtel Le Kaïla, which holds a Michelin star in the current Michelin Guide. La Coursive des Alpes, also in the Michelin selection, serves modern cooking in the village. Tables in high season, and especially over Christmas, New Year and February, go early, so we reserve as soon as your dates are known.

At lunchtime, many guests ski over from Courchevel for a meal on a terrace in the Méribel valley and ski back in the afternoon. We book the table, suggest a route that matches the group's level and keep a driver on standby if the weather closes the lifts.
