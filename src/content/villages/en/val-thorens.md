## Val Thorens as seen from Courchevel

Val Thorens sits at the head of the Belleville valley, the third of the Trois Vallées, at around 2,300 m. It is the highest resort in the area and is often described as the highest ski resort in Europe. Built above the tree line, it is a compact, mostly car-free resort surrounded by high, open slopes and glaciers.

For guests staying in Courchevel, Val Thorens is usually a day out rather than a base: a long ski day across the Trois Vallées, an ice driving session, or a lunch in a different setting. Some groups also split their stay, with a few nights in Courchevel and a few at altitude in Val Thorens.

## Getting there from Courchevel

**On skis.** The crossing goes through the Méribel valley. From Courchevel the lifts lead over La Saulire or the Col de la Loze into [Méribel](/meribel/), then on to Méribel-Mottaret and up towards Mont de la Chambre, from where pistes run down into Val Thorens. For a confident intermediate skier, the most direct route takes a little over an hour each way, and more in busy weeks or with stops. Leave Val Thorens early enough to catch the lifts back to Courchevel before they close; missing the last connection means a long journey by road. A Trois Vallées pass is needed.

**By road.** The drive goes down from Courchevel to Moûtiers, then climbs the Belleville valley on the D117 through Saint-Martin-de-Belleville and Les Menuires, 37 km from Moûtiers to Val Thorens. From Courchevel 1850 that is about 60 km, or around an hour and a quarter in good conditions. Snow and Saturday traffic add time. Winter tyres or chains are compulsory in Savoie's mountain areas from 1 November to 31 March, and every car we book is equipped. For the journey to the resort from airports and stations, see [getting to Courchevel](/guides/getting-to-courchevel/).

## Who Val Thorens suits

Val Thorens suits strong skiers who want long, high runs, groups looking for a change from their usual pistes, and anyone who wants to try ice driving. Its altitude often means good snow early and late in the season, when lower slopes are thinner.

It is also exposed. On grey or windy days, visibility above the tree line can be poor, and some lifts may close. On those days we usually suggest staying on the forested slopes around [La Tania](/la-tania/) or [Courchevel 1550 Village](/courchevel-1550-village/), and moving the Val Thorens day to a clear morning.

## Ice driving and other activities

Val Thorens has an ice driving circuit of around 800 m at about 2,200 m altitude, one of the highest in Europe. A session with an instructor teaches car control on ice and snow. We book the session, arrange a chauffeur for the journey there and back, and fit it around your ski days. See our [experiences](/services/experiences/) for other activities across the Trois Vallées.

## Where to eat

We reserve a table for guests who want to dine in Val Thorens and arrange the transfer back to Courchevel afterwards, so nobody needs to drive the valley road at night. For ski days, we book lunch on the slopes on the way over or back, chosen to fit the route.
