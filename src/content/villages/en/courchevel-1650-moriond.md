## Who it suits

Moriond sits on a sunny, open balcony above the valley, a little below Courchevel 1850. The atmosphere is relaxed and convivial, with bars and restaurants that are lively in the evening without being overwhelming. The village is lived in all year round, which gives it a sense of everyday life.

It suits families with children who are learning or building confidence, groups of friends who prefer a slower pace, and anyone who likes the idea of a sunny terrace at the end of the day. You stay close to 1850 for an evening out, then come back to a quieter village.

Moriond is also close to Aquamotion, the resort's large aquatic and wellness centre, which sits between Moriond and Courchevel Village. On a grey afternoon it gives children a pool to look forward to and adults a sauna and treatments, a few minutes from the chalet.

## Getting there

**By road.** Moriond lies on the road up to Courchevel. Guide times to the resort are about 150 km and 2 h 15 from Geneva airport, about 187 km and 2 h to 2 h 15 from Lyon Saint-Exupéry, and about 110 km and 1 h 20 from Chambéry. The nearest railway station is Moûtiers, around 30 minutes below the resort. Snow and Saturday changeover traffic add time, so we plan arrivals around them.

**By air.** The Courchevel altiport, above 1850, takes helicopters and small aircraft in daylight and suitable weather. From there a car brings you down to Moriond in a few minutes.

**Between villages.** In winter you can ski from Moriond to the slopes above 1850 and back. Free shuttles link Moriond with the other Courchevel villages, so a car is not essential, though we can arrange a private driver for evenings.

## Skiing from the village

Moriond has its own ski sector, reached from the village by the Ariondaz gondola. Its slopes are known for being wide, sunny and well suited to beginners and intermediate skiers, which is why so many families choose it. From the top of the sector you connect with the slopes above 1850, and from there with Méribel and the rest of the Trois Vallées.

The village's sunny aspect is pleasant in mid-winter. Later in the season, we suggest skiing the higher, shadier slopes in the morning and coming back to Moriond for lunch.

## Where to eat

At the top of the Ariondaz gondola, Le Bel Air is a long-established mountain restaurant with a large sunny terrace and traditional Savoyard dishes; it fills quickly, so we book ahead. Nearby, the Ferme-Auberge de l'Ariondaz serves simple mountain cooking at lunchtime.

In the village itself you will find relaxed restaurants serving Savoyard specialities such as fondue and raclette, alongside pizzerias and bars for après-ski. For a more formal dinner, the Michelin-starred tables of 1850 are a short drive away, and we can arrange the car there and back.

Many families prefer to eat in on some evenings. A [private chef](/services/private-chef/) can take care of breakfasts, children's meals and dinners at the chalet.
