## Who it suits

Courchevel 1850 is the heart of the resort and the village most people mean when they say "Courchevel". It suits guests who want the slopes, the restaurants and the shops on their doorstep: couples, groups of friends and families who like an active evening as much as a full day on the mountain. Most of the palace and five-star hotels are here, along with many of the largest chalets, set in the forest around the Jardin Alpin and along the roads towards the altiport.

It is also the busiest village, particularly over Christmas, New Year and the February holidays. If you would rather come back to a quiet chalet each evening, the lower villages offer that, and they remain connected to 1850 by lift.

## Getting there

**By road.** Courchevel is about 150 km from Geneva airport (2 h 15 in normal conditions), 187 km from Lyon Saint-Exupéry (2 h to 2 h 15) and 110 km from Chambéry (1 h 20). The last stretch climbs from Moûtiers, about 25 km and 30 minutes below the resort, which is also the nearest railway station. Snow and Saturday traffic can lengthen every one of these times, so we plan departures around them.

**By air.** Courchevel Altiport sits at about 2,000 m, just above the village. Its short, steep runway takes helicopters and small aircraft flown by specially qualified pilots, and flights only operate in daylight and suitable weather. A helicopter from Geneva takes about 30 to 35 minutes in the air. We arrange the flight, the luggage and a car waiting at the altiport, with a road transfer on standby.

**Between villages.** The Praz gondola climbs from Le Praz to 1850 in about six minutes, and the Grangettes gondola links Courchevel Village with 1850 in a few minutes. Moriond and La Tania are connected by the slopes, and free shuttles run between all the villages.

## Skiing from the village

The main lifts leave from the snow front at the Croisette, at the centre of the village. From there you reach the Saulire, the Verdons and the high slopes above the altiport, and from the Saulire you can continue towards Méribel and the rest of the Trois Vallées. The Courchevel valley has around 150 km of pistes on its own, and the Trois Vallées around 600 km. Our page on [the Courchevel ski area](/courchevel-ski-area/) follows the slopes from the summit down to Le Praz.

Several of the ski schools have meeting points on the snow front, which makes mornings simpler with children. For stronger skiers, we arrange private instructors and mountain guides for off-piste days, with equipment fitted at the chalet the evening before.

## Where to eat

1850 has the highest concentration of fine dining in the resort. Its Michelin-starred tables include Le 1947 à Cheval Blanc, Le Chabichou and L'Altitude; our guide to [where to eat in Courchevel](/guides/where-to-eat/) lists them all for this season. Tables for the festive weeks are in demand, so we book as soon as your dates are confirmed.

For lunch on the mountain, Le Chalet de Pierres on the Verdons piste is a long-standing address for traditional Savoyard cooking on a sunny terrace. In the village, there is everything from relaxed brasseries to late-night restaurants with music, and we can suggest the right place for the evening you have in mind.

If you would rather stay in, a [private chef](/services/private-chef/) can cook at the chalet, from a simple family supper to a tasting menu for a celebration.
